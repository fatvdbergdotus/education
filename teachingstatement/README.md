# Teaching Statement

## 1. Teaching Philosophy

My teaching philosophy is based on the belief that education is a shared, continuous process of learning, exploration, and personal development. As a teacher, I aim to help students develop not only the knowledge and technical skills required in their field, but also the ability to think critically, solve problems independently, reflect on their work, and continue learning throughout their careers.

My academic journey, which includes two Master's degrees, a PhD, and three postdoctoral research appointments, has strongly influenced my perspective on education. Having studied and conducted research at different academic institutions, I have experienced how knowledge develops across disciplines and how important curiosity, critical thinking, and intellectual independence are to academic and professional growth. This background has also taught me that learning does not end with obtaining a degree; it is a continuous process of questioning, discovering, and refining one's understanding.

One principle that strongly influences my approach to education is: **“The more you know, the more you realise how much you don't know.”** I believe that acquiring knowledge should not only provide answers but also stimulate curiosity and an awareness of what remains to be discovered. I therefore encourage students to question assumptions, explore alternative perspectives, and remain open to new insights. Education should help students become confident in what they know while remaining curious and humble about what they have yet to learn.

I consider a safe and supportive learning environment essential for this process. Students should feel comfortable asking questions, expressing uncertainty, challenging ideas, experimenting, and making mistakes without fear of embarrassment or judgement. Mistakes are a natural and valuable part of learning. This principle also applies to teachers: I do not consider myself infallible, and I believe that acknowledging my own mistakes, correcting misunderstandings, and learning from feedback demonstrates the attitude I want students to develop. A good learning environment is one in which both students and teachers can learn from one another.

Ultimately, my goal is to create an environment in which students feel supported and challenged, develop confidence in their abilities, and become independent, responsible, and lifelong learners.

## 2. Teaching Experience and Educational Approach

My teaching experience includes working as a lecturer at Fontys University of Applied Sciences, where I taught Software Engineering, Functional Programming, and Systems Thinking between 2021 and 2023. These subjects allowed me to combine theoretical foundations with practical applications and to help students understand how different areas of computer science relate to real-world problems.

In addition to lecturing, I have experience organising workshops, assessing student work, and supervising 17 internship students. These activities have given me insight into the different ways students learn and the importance of adapting educational approaches to their needs, backgrounds, and levels of experience.

At Radboud University, I gained additional experience as a Student Assistant and Student Assessor. These roles provided me with perspectives on educational support, student experiences, and the evaluation of education. They also strengthened my appreciation of the importance of student involvement in educational development and quality assurance.

My academic background consists of two Master's degrees, a PhD, and three postdoctoral research appointments. This extensive academic experience has provided me with a broad foundation in research, scientific reasoning, and interdisciplinary collaboration. It has also given me experience in approaching complex problems, evaluating existing knowledge, and communicating scientific findings. I consider these skills highly relevant to higher education, where students should learn not only established theories and methods but also how to evaluate, question, and extend them.

My approach to teaching combines structured instruction with opportunities for independent exploration. Depending on the learning objectives and the students' needs, I make use of course-based, demand-based, research-based, and open learning approaches. I believe that effective education requires a thoughtful balance between guidance and independence.

## 3. Student-Centred and Research-Informed Learning

I believe that students learn most effectively when they are actively involved in the learning process. Rather than relying exclusively on lectures and the transfer of information, I aim to create opportunities for students to apply concepts, investigate questions, discuss ideas, and develop their own solutions.

I use different educational approaches depending on the context:

- **Course-based learning:** I use structured teaching to establish a common foundation of knowledge and ensure that students acquire the essential concepts and skills required for a subject.
- **Demand-based learning:** I recognise that students have different backgrounds, interests, and learning needs. Where appropriate, I adapt activities and provide opportunities for students to focus on individual learning objectives.
- **Research-based learning:** I encourage students to investigate open-ended problems, formulate questions, evaluate evidence, and develop solutions based on systematic reasoning.
- **Open learning:** I value opportunities for students to take responsibility for their learning, explore topics independently, and develop their own interests.

I consider research-informed teaching particularly important in higher education. My PhD and three postdoctoral research appointments have given me extensive experience with the research process, from identifying research questions and reviewing existing literature to designing investigations, evaluating results, and communicating findings. I aim to bring this experience into the classroom by showing students how scientific knowledge is developed and why established knowledge should be examined critically.

By introducing students to research questions and current developments, I aim to demonstrate that computer science is not simply a collection of established techniques, but a discipline in which new questions and solutions continually emerge.

## 4. Connecting Theory and Practice

An important aspect of my teaching is connecting theoretical knowledge to practical applications. Students should understand not only how a concept or technique works, but also why it is relevant and how it can be applied to real-world problems.

My academic and professional experience provides opportunities to make these connections. I have worked at Wageningen University, the University of Twente, Eindhoven University of Technology, and TNO-ESI. At the University of Twente, my work included research into the automated performance evaluation of service-oriented systems, including applications related to Philips Healthcare medical imaging.

Having completed two Master's degrees and a PhD, followed by three postdoctoral research appointments, I have gained experience in approaching problems from different academic and research perspectives. This interdisciplinary background enables me to illustrate how theoretical concepts can be applied in different contexts and how knowledge from one field can contribute to solving problems in another.

In computer science education, for example, theoretical concepts such as software architecture, algorithms, programming paradigms, and performance evaluation become more meaningful when students can relate them to actual systems and engineering challenges.

I aim to use practical assignments, workshops, programming exercises, case studies, and project-based activities to help students develop the ability to transfer theoretical knowledge into practical solutions.

## 5. Assessment and Student Development

I consider assessment an integral part of the learning process rather than merely a mechanism for assigning grades. Effective assessment should provide insight into students' progress, identify areas for improvement, and support the achievement of learning objectives.

I distinguish between three complementary forms of assessment:

- **Diagnostic assessment:** Used to identify students' prior knowledge, skills, and potential learning gaps before or during a learning activity.
- **Formative assessment:** Used throughout the learning process to provide feedback and help students improve their understanding and performance.
- **Summative assessment:** Used to evaluate whether students have achieved the intended learning outcomes at the end of a course or learning period.

I aim to make assessment criteria transparent and ensure that students understand what is expected of them. Feedback should be constructive, specific, and actionable, allowing students to understand both what they have done well and how they can improve.

My experience as a Student Assessor at Radboud University has reinforced my appreciation of the importance of student perspectives in evaluating educational quality. I believe that students should have opportunities to contribute to discussions about teaching, assessment, and the learning environment.

When supervising students, particularly during internships and project-based assignments, I aim to find an appropriate balance between providing direction and encouraging independence. My experience supervising 17 internship students has taught me that students benefit from regular feedback, clear expectations, and sufficient freedom to take ownership of their work.

I also believe that mistakes should be treated as opportunities for reflection and improvement. Students should be able to discuss difficulties openly and understand that learning involves experimentation and sometimes failure. Teachers should demonstrate the same openness by acknowledging their own mistakes and being receptive to feedback.

## 6. Continuous Professional Development as a Teacher

I regard teaching as a profession that requires continuous reflection and development. Educational methods, technologies, student expectations, and disciplinary knowledge evolve, and teachers should be willing to adapt their approaches accordingly.

My own academic development, from two Master's degrees to a PhD and three postdoctoral research appointments, has reinforced the importance of lifelong learning. Each stage of my academic career has required me to acquire new knowledge, reconsider existing assumptions, and develop new skills. I believe that this attitude is equally important in teaching.

I use reflection to evaluate the effectiveness of my teaching activities and identify opportunities for improvement. I consider student feedback, assessment results, discussions with colleagues, and my own observations valuable sources of information.

The ALACT model of reflection, developed by Fred Korthagen, provides a useful structure for this process:

1. **Action:** Carry out a teaching activity.
2. **Looking back:** Reflect on what happened.
3. **Awareness:** Identify important aspects, challenges, and underlying assumptions.
4. **Creating alternatives:** Consider different approaches and possible improvements.
5. **Trial:** Apply an alternative approach and evaluate its effectiveness.

This process encourages me to approach teaching as an ongoing learning activity. Just as I expect students to reflect on their development, I believe teachers should regularly question their assumptions, evaluate their methods, and remain open to new perspectives.

I also value collaboration with colleagues and the exchange of educational experiences. Discussing teaching methods, assessment strategies, and student challenges can provide valuable insights and contribute to the continuous improvement of education.

## 7. Concluding Statement

My ambition as a teacher is to contribute to an educational environment in which students develop strong disciplinary knowledge, practical skills, critical thinking, and the confidence to explore unfamiliar problems. My academic background, comprising two Master's degrees, a PhD, and three postdoctoral research appointments, together with my teaching and professional experience, allows me to bring academic depth, research experience, and practical perspectives into the classroom.

I aim to combine structured instruction with student-centred, research-informed, and practice-oriented learning. Above all, I want students to experience education as a safe and stimulating environment in which questions are encouraged, mistakes are accepted as part of learning, and curiosity is valued.

I believe that the most effective teachers do not simply transfer knowledge; they inspire students to keep learning, to question what they know, and to discover what they do not yet know. I see myself as part of that learning process. By remaining reflective, open to feedback, and willing to learn from both students and colleagues, I aim to continue developing as an educator while helping students become independent, thoughtful, and lifelong learners.
